Trading Performance Analytics

A journal tells you what happened. Analytics tells you what it means. BattlexJournal computes the standard performance metrics — win rate, profit factor, expectancy, average R, drawdown and equity curve — from your journal, with breakdowns by strategy, setup, session and symbol.

The core metrics

Every metric is computed from your journal ledger, using the same definitions across the app:

  • Win rate — wins divided by wins plus losses. Useful, but never judge your trading by it alone.
  • Profit factor — gross profit divided by gross loss. A profit factor above 1 means you make more than you give back; above 1.5–2 is the range most traders target.
  • Expectancy — the average result per trade, expressed as average R. This is the metric that combines win rate and payoff: a low win rate with high R winners can still be strongly positive.
  • Average R — average result in multiples of risk, the unit that makes trades comparable across instruments and account sizes.
  • Max drawdown — the largest peak-to-trough decline in your equity curve. The honest cost of your process.
  • Recovery — net profit divided by max drawdown: how much you earn for every unit of pain you endure.
  • Equity curve — your balance over time, the one chart that cannot lie about whether the process is working.

Breakdowns that find the edge

Averages hide more than they reveal. BattlexJournal breaks the same metrics down by the dimensions you record on every trade:

  • By strategy — which of your strategies is actually paying, and which is quietly leaking.
  • By setup — the pattern-level view: your A+ setup versus the setups you take because the chart "looked right".
  • By session — often the biggest split in a journal: the same approach that wins in London and loses in Asia.
  • By symbol — performance per instrument, so winners and laggards are explicit.
  • By direction — long versus short performance, which matters for trend-driven systems.
  • Streaks — win and loss streaks, so you can see how you behave after a stretch of losses.
  • Risk buckets — how performance and risk behave at different risk levels, exposing escalation patterns.

Filters, not just reports

Analytics are filterable by symbol, setup, session, direction, result, emotion and adherence — so you can ask specific questions of your history: "How does my London FVG setup perform when I'm in New York?" or "What do my trades after a loss look like?" The answers come from the same engine every screen reads, so numbers never disagree between views.

Analytics only as good as the journal

Analytics derive entirely from your journal. If you are consistent with sessions and setups, the breakdowns are meaningful; if not, the gaps show up as weak or missing data. The journal template shows the fields to keep. Importing an existing history — CSV, Excel, Google Sheets or Notion — makes analytics meaningful immediately instead of after weeks of logging.

How risk and discipline fit in

Performance only makes sense next to the risk that produced it. The same ledger feeds risk management (drawdown and limits) and discipline (rule adherence), so you can always ask whether the results came from the process — or despite it.
