Trading Journal for Futures Traders

Journal futures trades with contract-aware risk and P&L tracking: entry and exit, ticks and points, risk per contract, session, setup and execution quality — reviewed against your own rules and measured over time.

What a futures journal records

Futures add a layer of precision that a general journal often ignores: contracts, tick sizes, point values and session-specific liquidity. A futures journal entry in BattlexJournal captures the standard fields plus the ones futures traders actually think in:

  • Contract and direction — ES, NQ, CL, GC and the rest, long or short.
  • Entry and exit prices — open, close, stop loss and take profit.
  • Position size in contracts — so risk and P&L are computed per contract with the instrument's contract value.
  • Risk — the dollar amount at risk, derived from entry, stop and size.
  • R multiple — the result in units of risk, so a 4-tick scalp and a 20-point swing are comparable.
  • Session and setup — open, RTH, overnight, and the pattern that triggered the entry.

Why futures traders need the extra structure

Futures are unforgiving to vague journaling. A few reasons the details matter:

  • Ticks add up. A single bad fill, a stop that was too tight, a size that was too large — each looks small in isolation and compounds across a month.
  • Session matters more than the chart. The same ES setup behaves differently in the open, the RTH midday, and the overnight session. Recording the session is how you find out.
  • Risk is per contract. Oversizing by one contract on a volatile instrument is the difference between a normal day and a blown account. The journal records the risk you actually took.
  • Execution quality is measurable. With entry, exit and stop recorded for every trade, slippage and bad fills become visible patterns instead of vague feelings.

Performance and risk analytics for futures

From a consistent futures journal, BattlexJournal computes the numbers that tell you whether the process is working:

  • Win rate, profit factor and expectancy — overall and per contract.
  • Performance by session and by setup, so the edge (or the leak) becomes visible.
  • Average R, drawdown and equity curve — see trading performance analytics for the complete metric list.
  • Risk usage against your own daily risk budget, loss limit and drawdown limit — enforced by the risk management engine.
  • Rule adherence and violations — scored in the discipline system.

Discipline in futures trading

Futures traders often fail on behavior, not analysis: revenge entries after a stop-out, sizing up after a win, trading into low-liquidity periods. Because BattlexJournal evaluates every trade against the rules you define, those behaviors are recorded as violations with the trade attached — and your discipline score tracks whether they are getting rarer.
