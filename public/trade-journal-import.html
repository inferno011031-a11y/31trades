Trade Journal Import

Switching to BattlexJournal should not mean retyping years of trades. The import tool brings supported historical journal formats into your ledger — with column mapping, row-by-row validation and duplicate detection before anything is committed.

What you can import

BattlexJournal imports tabular trade history from the formats most traders already keep their journal in:

  • CSV files — comma- or tab-delimited exports from spreadsheets and journaling tools.
  • Excel files (.xlsx) — parsed structurally, worksheet by worksheet.
  • Google Sheets exports — the CSV export of a Google Sheets journal.
  • Notion exports — the CSV export of a Notion database or table used as a journal.
  • Pasted tabular data — copy rows straight from a spreadsheet and paste them in.

These are the formats the import engine currently supports. Broker-specific exports or proprietary formats are not supported as native integrations — but if your broker can export a CSV or Excel file, you can import that.

How the import works

  1. Upload or paste. Drop your file in the Import page (or paste rows). Files are validated before parsing — size, extension and content are checked first.
  2. Column detection. BattlexJournal reads your header row and maps recognizable columns — date, symbol, direction, entry, exit, stop, risk, P&L, setup, session and more. Anything it cannot confidently identify is left unmapped rather than guessed.
  3. Row-by-row validation. Every row is normalized and checked. Invalid rows are flagged with the specific problem (bad date, missing symbol, unparsable P&L) instead of silently corrupting your history.
  4. Duplicate detection. Rows that match trades already in your ledger are identified before commit, so re-importing the same file does not double your history.
  5. Review, then commit. You see the preview — mapped columns, row states, errors, warnings and duplicates — and only commit when it looks right. Nothing touches your ledger until you confirm.

Why import instead of starting fresh

Your historical trades are the evidence base for everything BattlexJournal computes: equity curve, win rate by setup, risk behavior, discipline scores and AI findings. Importing your history means analytics are meaningful from day one instead of after weeks of manual logging.

The import lands in your normal journal and flows through the same engine as manually logged trades — risk, discipline and analytics all update from the imported ledger.

Private by design. Imported journals are yours alone. Import batches, row previews and trade data are stored per user, are never published, and never appear on the public site or in the sitemap.

Keep it consistent

An import is only as good as the source journal. If you are still building your column structure, the trading journal template lists the fields worth keeping, and the journaling guide explains why each one matters.
