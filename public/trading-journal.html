Trading Journal

A trading journal is the record of every decision you make in the market — what you traded, why, how much you risked, and what happened. BattlexJournal turns that record into a connected system of review, discipline and performance analysis.

What is a trading journal?

A trading journal is a log of your trades. At its simplest, it answers five questions about every trade: what you traded, when, which direction, at what price, and with what result. Most traders quickly add a sixth question that separates good journals from bad ones: why you took the trade.

That "why" — the setup you saw, the session you were in, the risk you planned, the notes you wrote while the trade was live — is what turns a list of prices into something you can actually learn from.

Why traders keep one

Markets are noisy. A single trade, or even a week of trades, tells you very little — one good setup can lose, and one bad habit can win. A journal lets you step back from the noise and answer questions your memory cannot:

  • Which setups actually make you money? Not which ones you think should.
  • Where does your risk actually go? Trades taken on emotion, revenge entries after a loss, oversized positions after a win streak.
  • Are you following your own rules? The journal is where rule-breaking becomes visible instead of forgettable.
  • Is your process improving? Win rate and profit factor change slowly; what changes first is consistency.

None of this requires special discipline as a personality trait. It requires a record you can measure — which is exactly what a journal is for.

What should be recorded

A useful journal entry captures the decision and the context around it. These are the fields BattlexJournal stores for every trade:

FieldWhat it captures
Date & timeWhen the trade was opened — the raw material for session analysis.
Symbol & directionWhat you traded (EURUSD, NQ, BTC…) and whether you were long or short.
Entry & exitFill prices, plus stop loss and take profit if you set them.
Risk & RDollar risk on the trade and the result expressed in R multiples — the unit that makes performance comparable across trades.
SessionWhich session you traded (London, New York, Asia…), since session is one of the strongest predictors of performance.
SetupThe pattern or condition that triggered the entry — so you can measure setups instead of guessing about them.
Strategy & rulesWhich of your strategies the trade belonged to, and which rules applied to it.
NotesYour own context: news, mood, why the trade looked good, what you were thinking.

How BattlexJournal handles journaling

BattlexJournal is built around the journal as the single source of truth. When you log a trade it:

  • Calculates the numbers for you — P&L, position size, risk and R are computed from your entry, exit and stop, with asset-aware pricing for forex, futures, indices, crypto and stocks.
  • Runs your rule set — each trade is evaluated against the rules you have defined, and violations or blocks are recorded next to the trade.
  • Updates everything downstream — your equity curve, risk state, discipline score and analytics all recompute from the same ledger, so every screen shows the same numbers.
  • Flags unreviewed trades — pending reviews are surfaced until you have reviewed the decision, not just the result.

You can log trades manually, or bring an existing history in with the trade journal import for CSV, Excel, Google Sheets and Notion exports.

Beyond the log: review, discipline, risk

A journal is only the first step. The value compounds when the same records feed the rest of your process:

  • Trading discipline — your rule adherence is scored across risk, strategy, execution, frequency, session and behavior, with violations tied back to the exact trade.
  • Trading risk management — daily risk budgets, loss limits and drawdown are tracked automatically against your journal history.
  • Trading performance analytics — win rate, profit factor, expectancy, average R, drawdown and equity curve, broken down by strategy, setup, session and symbol.
  • Trading replay — practice new ideas on historical market data before they touch your journal.
