Trading Risk Management Basics: Position Sizing, Daily Limits, Drawdown

August 18, 2026 · BattlexJournal

Risk management is the part of trading you control completely. These are the core numbers — risk per trade, daily budgets, drawdown, frequency — and how to track them without a spreadsheet.

Risk per trade

The foundation is the dollar amount you are willing to lose on a single trade, defined before entry: risk = (entry − stop) × position size. A common starting rule is a fixed percentage of the account — the exact number depends on your strategy and tolerance, not on a universal formula. What matters is that it is decided before the trade and applied to every trade.

Daily risk budget and daily loss limit

Two numbers protect the day:

  • Daily risk budget — the total risk you plan to deploy in a day. When it is used up, the day is done.
  • Daily loss limit — the maximum loss you allow before you stop trading entirely for the day. It is the circuit breaker for tilt: a losing morning cannot become a losing week.

BattlexJournal tracks used and remaining amounts for both from your journal, and refuses to pass a hard limit when you plan a new trade — see trading risk management.

Drawdown

Drawdown is the distance from your equity peak to its current trough. Tracking it matters because it is the metric that ends accounts (and evaluations): a 10% drawdown requires an 11% gain to recover; a 50% drawdown requires 100%. Set a drawdown limit you are willing to live with, and treat hitting it as a system problem, not a bad week.

Trade frequency

The least tracked risk is how often you trade. More trades mean more commission, more opportunity for emotional entries, and more chances to give back a good week in one afternoon. Set a maximum trade count per day from your plan — not from the market — and record the actual count. Frequency is a scored dimension in trading discipline, so overtrading shows up in the score.

Why limits fail in practice

Most traders know these numbers. The limits fail for mechanical reasons: the math is done by hand, the tracking stops after a few days, the numbers live in a file nobody opens mid-session. The fix is not more willpower — it is moving the checks to the moment of entry. A pre-trade check that evaluates each planned trade against your limits, before you commit, is the difference between having rules and enforcing them.

From rules to review

Limits are a starting point, not a conclusion. The weekly review should include how often you approached each limit — if you are always at the edge of the daily loss limit, either the limit or the behavior needs to change.
