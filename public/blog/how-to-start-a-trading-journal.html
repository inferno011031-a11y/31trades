How to Start a Trading Journal (Step by Step)

August 18, 2026 · BattlexJournal

Starting a trading journal feels like a big project. It isn't. This is the smallest journal that actually works, in five steps.

Step 1: Decide what you are tracking

You do not need a perfect schema on day one. You need five fields per trade: what you traded, when, which direction, the prices (entry, exit, stop), and why you took it. Add session, setup and risk within the first week — they are the fields that unlock the analysis later. The full list lives on the trading journal template page.

Step 2: Make logging faster than forgetting

The journal fails when logging feels like homework. Three ways to keep it fast:

  • Log the same day. Same-day notes are accurate; end-of-week notes are fiction.
  • Use a template. The same fields in the same order every time — no decisions about structure while you are logging.
  • Let the tool calculate. P&L, risk and R are math, not reflection. A journaling tool that computes them removes the most tedious part.

Step 3: Write notes for the trade, not for an audience

The note field is where the learning lives. Good notes answer one question: what was I seeing, and what did I decide to do about it? They do not need to be long. "FVG filled at open, news in 20 minutes, took it anyway" is a complete note — it will mean everything to you in next month's review.

Step 4: Review on a schedule

Recording without reviewing is collecting. Start with a five-minute daily review of the day's trades against your rules, and a weekly review of the numbers. You are looking for one thing to change per week — not a personality overhaul. The full framework is in how to review your trades.

Step 5: Let the record set your limits

After three or four weeks you have data. Use it: set a daily risk budget and loss limit from what your journal shows you actually risk, define rules from the trades that followed your plan, and let your analytics show which setups and sessions pay. This is the moment a journal stops being a record and becomes a system.

The minimum viable journal

If you only do one thing today: log your next trade — five fields, one note — and review it tonight. That is a trading journal. Everything else is refinement.
