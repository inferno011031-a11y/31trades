How to Review Your Trades Like a Trader Who Actually Improves

August 18, 2026 · BattlexJournal

Reviewing trades is the difference between journaling and learning. This is a repeatable framework: score the decision, separate process from outcome, find the single thing to fix, and turn it into a rule.

Review the decision, not just the P&L

The result of a trade is mostly out of your control by the time you review it. The decision was fully in your control. So the review starts with the decision:

  • Did the trade match a defined setup from your plan?
  • Was the risk within your limits — position size, stop placement, daily budget?
  • Did you execute the plan as defined, or improvise?
  • Were you in your planned session, timeframe and strategy?

Answering these four questions on every trade gives you an adherence record — the raw material of trading discipline. BattlexJournal answers them automatically by evaluating each trade against your rule set, but the questions are the same whether you score by hand or by tool.

Separate process from outcome

The hard part is resisting outcome bias. A perfect decision can lose, and a terrible one can win — markets are probabilistic. The review must record both dimensions honestly:

  • Good process, losing trade: nothing to fix. Mark the trade reviewed and move on.
  • Bad process, winning trade: the most dangerous trade in your journal. It looks fine and teaches the wrong lesson. This is the one to study.
  • Bad process, losing trade: the obvious one. Identify which rule broke.
  • Good process, winning trade: the one to replicate. Note what made it a clean execution.

Find one thing to change

A review that produces a list of twenty changes produces none. Weekly, pick the single most frequent or most expensive issue — the setup that keeps losing, the session where you keep breaking rules, the sizing pattern after losses — and make it the focus of the next week. Your analytics make this choice concrete: see trading performance analytics for the breakdowns by setup, session and strategy.

Turn the fix into a rule

A fix that is not written down evaporates by Tuesday. Turn each review conclusion into a measurable rule — "no new entries within 30 minutes of a stop-out", "max 3 trades per session", "no size above 1.5× average risk" — and evaluate trades against it. That is exactly what the rule engine does on every logged trade, so adherence becomes a score you can watch improve.

Review on a cadence

  • Daily (5 minutes): score today's trades against your rules; mark the reviewed ones.
  • Weekly (30 minutes): look at the week's metrics and discipline score; choose the one focus for next week.
  • Monthly (1 hour): check whether your edge still holds by setup and session, and whether your risk is in balance with your results.

BattlexJournal derives daily, weekly and monthly reviews from your journal history and surfaces unreviewed trades until you close the loop — the review is part of the system, not a separate habit to remember.
